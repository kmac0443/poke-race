

Yes. I think we're getting toward a much more coherent game loop now.

The key insight is that the training minigames shouldn't themselves be the ultimate game. They're the equivalent of lifting weights, running drills, practicing swordsmanship, etc. The race/triathlon is where the child gets to see what all that training accomplished.

And I really like your idea that the three stats interact rather than simply having three independent competitions.

Yes. I think this is starting to become a very coherent game system, rather than a collection of math minigames.

The big idea I'd lock in is:

The child's math ability determines the difficulty. The creature's stats determine how demanding the game becomes. Training makes the creature stronger, and races demonstrate what that training accomplished.

And I really like the restriction that the child never types an answer. The keyboard is for controlling the creature, while the math determines what the child should do.

1. Math complexity should be a combination of stats
I think your model works well.

You could have something conceptually like:

Overall difficulty = f(Speed + Strength + Defense)

but each training game has a difficulty floor/cap based on its own stat.

For example:

Total stats	Potential math
0–30	addition/subtraction within 12
30–60	multiplication 1–5, simple division
60–100	multiplication 1–12, division
100–150	mixed multiplication/division/fractions
150+	more difficult fractions/decimals
But Speed Training might say:

"I can only handle complexity up to level 2 until Speed reaches 30."

So even if:

Total = 100

but:

Speed = 15

Speed training remains relatively easy.

Meanwhile:

Strength = 50

means Strength training can give much harder problems.

That creates a nice reason to train all three stats.

2. I'd actually separate "math complexity" from "problem family"
This is important because you don't want the game to accidentally turn into a general math curriculum.

Your initial problem families could be very constrained:

Addition
4 + 7

9 + 3

11 + 8 perhaps later

Subtraction
12 - 5

17 - 9

etc.

Multiplication
3 × 7

8 × 4

eventually full:

1–12 × 1–12

Division
Simple facts:

24 ÷ 6

36 ÷ 4

etc.

Fractions
Probably visual initially:

½ + ¼

or identifying:

Which is larger?

½ vs ¼

Decimals
Eventually:

0.5 + 0.25

etc.

But the complexity system chooses which family and difficulty, rather than every training game needing to handle every kind of math.
